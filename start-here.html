After the film / a short reading guide

You Can See Everything explained: start here

Turn a confusing viewing experience into questions you can check.

Edited by the Theranos Film project · Briar assisted · Updated 21 November 2026. Editorial draft; not independently fact-checked. About this independent project · Corrections and review history.

Start with three separate questions

  1. What kind of material am I watching: recording, archive, performance or reconstruction?
  2. What exactly is being claimed, and about which date?
  3. Which source could establish or contradict that particular claim?

Spoiler notice: this guide discusses the film's performance element. It is written for viewers who have finished watching. It is not a verified scene-by-scene synopsis.

Why the two halves can feel confusing

The festival synopsis describes documentary access to Holmes and Evans; a participant interview discusses Seyfried's performance. These support distinguishing documentary access from performance, but do not establish a clean boundary at the halfway point. Do not assume every earlier shot is unconstructed or every later shot is acted.

Questions after watching You Can See Everything

Is Elizabeth Holmes played by an actor?

The production accounts distinguish access to Holmes from Amanda Seyfried's later performance. Check the passage, not simply which half it appears in. Read the real-footage and actors guide.

Who is the interviewer?

Nathan Fielder co-directed the film with Lance Oppenheim. Read Fielder's role and the account of how he became involved.

What did the Theranos machine actually do?

A working assay, a multi-method prototype and a validated patient-testing service are different achievements. Compare Edison, miniLab and the documented limits.

Were investors buying a future idea or an existing invention?

Both forecasts and claims about present operations appear in the records. Follow the dated investor comparison.

What does the ending prove?

A later interview or reconstruction does not settle historical technical claims. Check the performance, memory and scene-evidence limits.

Where did the money go?

We have some historical expense records, not a complete spending ledger. Inspect the financial reconstruction and separate settlement payments.

A short route through the investigation

  1. Meet the people: separate company executives, later personal relationships and the people constructing the film.
  2. Check the recording and performance guide: identify what a passage can establish before interpreting it.
  3. Compare present capability with future potential: read the investor wording and its limits.
  4. Understand the technology: collection, measurement, integration and validation are different achievements.
  5. Follow the money questions: distinguish investment, valuation, revenue and actual expenditure.
  6. Inspect the sources and submit a traceable correction.

What the film cannot settle by itself

A personal interview may illuminate someone's account without validating an instrument or reconstructing an investment decision. A performance can examine that account without becoming historical footage. Editing, sympathy, discomfort and visible behaviour do not determine whether a particular technical or financial statement was true.

What we can say about the legal result

The DOJ's January 2022 account reports four investor-related convictions for Holmes, together with acquittals and unresolved counts. Do not describe that mixed verdict as either total exoneration or proof of every accusation. This guide is not a current docket or appeal-status service.
