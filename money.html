We do not yet have a complete spending ledger

The sources support some fundraising and legal statements. They do not justify a pie chart claiming where all the money went. This page separates known source accounts from the records still needed.

Four different financial quantities
QuantityMeaningDo not confuse it with
Investment receiptsCash received through financing.Sales revenue or expenditure.
ValuationAn implied or assessed value of shares/company.Money sitting in the company's bank.
RevenueIncome recognised from customers.A forecast, fundraising receipt or profit.
ExpenditureResources spent, with cash and accrual treatments distinguished.Investment losses, restitution or a company's valuation.

Reported in a civil enforcement announcement

Capital raised

The SEC said Theranos raised more than $700 million from investors through the alleged fraud. This is a fundraising statement, not a reconstruction of the company's total lifetime receipts or expenditure.

Records needed: Dated cash receipts, investment instruments, opening and closing cash, and reconciliation to the precise period and scope of the figure.

Civil allegations; not audited accounts

Revenue forecasts and actual revenue

The SEC announcement alleges misleading business and revenue representations. Revenue is income from customers; investment receipts and share valuation belong in different categories.

Records needed: Exact forecast version and date, supporting assumptions, period-matched recognised revenue and the records available to the investor at the time.

Selected 2011–2013 expense testimony quantified; complete cash allocation unavailable

Actual expenditure

Spivey's testimony about Defence Exhibit 4176 gives operating expenses/R&D of $28.2m/$22m in 2011, about $67m/$53m in 2012 and about $92m/$68m in 2013. R&D is part of operating expenses, not an amount to add on top. These are testified accounting expenses, not a complete payment ledger or independently audited allocation. See the financial-records chapter for exact transcript locations and source limits.

Records needed: Period-matched general ledger, bank reconciliation, payroll summaries, supplier invoices, fixed-asset records and reliable public filings. Do not publish private account or employee details.

Editorial distinctions supported by different evidence types

What does failure mean?

Scientific feasibility, assay performance, service reliability, business viability, honest fundraising and legal liability are separate questions. The DOJ reported investor-fraud convictions; a JCI service comparison examines a narrower testing question. Neither is a complete cash ledger.

Records needed: Define the claimed failure before selecting evidence. A failed business is not by itself fraud; a fraud conviction does not settle whether every underlying scientific concept is impossible.

Reconcile cash before allocating blame

For one consistent period: opening cash + financing receipts + operating cash receipts + asset-sale proceeds − operating payments − capital payments − financing repayments = closing cash. Reconcile transfers and accounting classifications so the same item is not counted twice.

Document estimates as estimates, identify their source and avoid adding overlapping reported totals. A court's loss or restitution figure answers a different question from the company's expenditure.

What a contribution should contain

Bring a public filing or reliable account with its reporting period, currency, exact page and definition of the amount. Explain whether it records paid cash, an accrual, a forecast or a third party's estimate. Do not upload private banking records, personal employee data or unauthorised document copies.
