Understanding You Can See Everything

The people.
The claims. The evidence.

This investigation asks how the film shapes our understanding of Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos. It is not a verdict on anyone’s personality. An interview, a performance and a contemporaneous document answer different questions.

Current limit: this is an initial research guide, not a completed film fact check. Programme listings and a participant interview establish some identities and production context. Scene-by-scene verification remains unfinished. Questions below are not allegations.

Document evidence available · film comparison pending

Elizabeth Holmes: replace “is she truthful?” with testable claims

We need the exact assertion, its date, its audience and what she could reasonably have known then. Accurately describing a future ambition is different from accurately describing a present capability. Each statement needs its own evidence.

Trial Exhibit 3696, page 3, describes technology that “runs comprehensive blood tests from a finger-stick”. That wording presents a capability, not simply an aspiration. It establishes what the slide says, not whether it worked, who received that version or how Holmes explains it in the film.

Background and involvement

Build a sourced chronology of her education, founding and leadership of Theranos, technical involvement, fundraising and legal proceedings. Separate her own account from colleagues’ accounts and judicial findings. Credentials alone do not establish competence or intent.

Next investigation

Transcribe relevant film assertions with timestamps and compare them with dated investor materials and technical records. Preserve qualifications and counterevidence. Nodding, vocal style and confidence cannot establish dishonesty.

Billy Evans identified · relationship history incomplete

Partners: a relationship is not a shared responsibility

“Partner” may mean a romantic partner, executive colleague, investor or later collaborator. Identify which people appear or are discussed, and when they were involved, before drawing connections.

Film at Lincoln Center identifies William “Billy” Evans as Holmes’s romantic partner and places him in the film’s domestic setting. That does not establish technical expertise or responsibility for Theranos’s earlier conduct.

A separate track concerns Ramesh “Sunny” Balwani. The DOJ role/verdict reference identifies roles including president and chief operating officer. Full source review and a dated relationship chronology remain necessary. Do not conflate Evans’s and Balwani’s involvement.

Background and involvement

Record each verified relationship, employment or ownership role, decision-making authority and period of involvement. Distinguish firsthand technical knowledge from financial, personal and retrospective knowledge. Attribute any documented interests rather than speculating about motives.

Next investigation

Identify people through credits and scenes, then compare their accounts with filings, testimony and contemporaneous records. Ask what each actually knew or controlled. Do not transfer one person’s conduct or legal findings to another through association.

Director credit supported · method analysis developing

The interviewer: investigate the method as well as the biography

FACT and Film at Lincoln Center credit Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim as directors. The festival programme places Fielder in the filmed home environment and identifies earlier work including The Rehearsal and The Curse.

Relevant background includes his comedy and screen work, credited production responsibilities and any public explanation of his method. Previous work provides context, not proof of intention in a particular scene.

Next investigation

Who initiated the project and selected the questions? What opportunities existed for follow-up, fact checking and response? How do editing, pauses or staged situations shape the audience’s interpretation? “Why was he asked?” stays open until an attributable production account answers it.

Amanda Seyfried identified through participant interview

The actress: identify the performance before interpreting it

A published conversation with Amanda Seyfried, Fielder and Oppenheim establishes her involvement. They describe showing her earlier footage and later making recreations. Fielder describes adjusting performances to his recollection. That is a participant’s account, not proof of verbatim accuracy.

The film therefore includes filmed encounters and later performance. That does not settle every shot’s recording status: recreated scenes are reconstructions, not direct footage of Holmes.

Background and involvement

Start with official credits. Establish the role, scenes and reliable accounts of preparation, direction and access to the real person. Keep the performer’s own biography separate from the person portrayed.

Next investigation

Annotate where the film identifies a performance or changes recording context. What is disclosed at the time, and what is revealed later? A convincing portrayal does not independently corroborate its dialogue. An acting choice does not establish Holmes’s behaviour or state of mind.

How we will compare the accounts

  1. Identify the speaker, performer and recording context.
  2. Record the exact assertion, scene locator and relevant historical date.
  3. Link the original source and exact supporting or challenging passage.
  4. Explain what the evidence establishes and what it does not.
  5. Preserve disagreements and corrections with contributor and reviewer attribution.

The SEC’s 2018 complaint, paragraphs 48–51, is a starting point for investigating investor materials. It contains allegations, not findings on every assertion. Court decisions, testimony and exhibits must each retain their own evidential status.
