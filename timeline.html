What did investors know—and when?

A promise, a demonstration and a later explanation belong at different points in the evidence.

Edited by the Theranos Film project · Briar assisted · Updated 21 November 2026. Editorial draft; not independently fact-checked. About this independent project · Corrections and review history.

This is a source-linked chronology, not a complete transaction ledger. Periods are labelled as periods; a year-only entry does not imply an exact day. A later account must not be silently substituted for a contemporaneous representation.

Why did people invest?

Do not infer a particular investor’s motives from the size of a cheque or a famous name. A documented pitch can suggest proposed attractions—market need, intellectual property, convenience, business forecasts or endorsements—but an explanation of actual reliance needs that investor’s records and testimony.

Where did the money go?

Money raised is not the amount spent, and valuation is not cash. A defensible reconciliation needs opening cash, dated financing receipts, operating and capital payments, asset-sale proceeds, liabilities and closing cash. Payroll, development, premises, retail rollout and legal costs are categories to investigate, not verified spending allocations. Read the expenditure framework and its gaps.
