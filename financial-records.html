Later evidence that the creditor assignment occurred

The 2023 ABC agreement's recital records a General Assignment executed on 12 September 2018. That advances the evidence beyond the closure letter's proposal. It is later contractual evidence, not inspection of the original assignment instrument or proof of the IP subsidiary's title chain. Those distinct instruments remain needed.

A $45m allowed claim is not a $45m recovery

ABC agreement sections 1–2 allow separate $45m unsecured Walgreens and class claims. The class distribution is specified as $1,331,094.88, due within ten days after the required approval; Walgreens's claim participates pro rata. The February 2024 order approves implementation, with ABC approval concerning class claims. These are claim and payment terms, not a receipt, total estate valuation or original spending ledger.

Walgreens's $44m is separate from Theranos's remaining estate

Definitions 36–37 in the Walgreens agreement require Walgreens to fund $44m in cash into the settlement fund. This is a separate payer and obligation from the ABC estate's class distribution. Neither belongs in a total of historical Theranos research or operating expenditure. Settlement funding, allowed claims, gross funds and net class receipts are different figures; completed payment and final deductions need their own evidence.

September 2018: what the company said was left

Taylor's 4 September 2018 letter, filed as Exhibit D, reports default under the Fortress credit facility, at least $60m owed to unsecured creditors and roughly $5m remaining cash net of fees/expenses. It anticipates no shareholder distribution. This is a contemporaneous company account, not audited balances, final creditor recoveries or a reconstruction of 2015–2018 spending. Funding minus this balance must not be called money spent.

A proposed wind-down is not proof of completed IP transfer

The same letter describes a possible Fortress settlement involving the IP subsidiary, an assignment for the benefit of creditors and dissolution. Its final page says board/shareholder consents will be sought. Contacts with over 80 potential sale counterparties and 17 NDAs reportedly produced no sale. The letter alone does not prove completed settlement, title transfer, final sale value or distributions; executed instruments and final accounts remain needed.

2014 and a short 2015 period: historical accounts located

Exhibit 5209 reproduces an Aranca historical income statement in $'000. Dec-14 FY-A shows $150,000 revenue, $100.031m operating costs, $7.095m depreciation and a $106.666m loss. Mar-15 / 2 Mth-A shows $14,000 revenue, $22.961m operating costs, $5.691m depreciation and a $28.648m loss. Preserve the unusual period labels: do not call this a complete quarter or annualise it. These are actual-labelled company figures reproduced in a valuation, not audited cash expenditure.

Cash balances are not a spending reconciliation

The next Aranca page reports cash/equivalents of $463.310m at Dec-14 and $557.081m at Mar-15. Financing and other cash movements can affect balances; the difference is not money spent. Depreciation, operating costs, loss, financing and closing cash are different accounting measures. Do not add depreciation or R&D twice, or silently reconcile earlier testimony and differing revenue definitions.

Keep forecasts, revenue and IRS total income distinct

The SEC complaint's $108m figure is an allegedly supplied investor financial, not actual revenue; it describes actual 2014 recorded revenue as little over $100,000. The valuation's $150,000 historical figure needs accounting/version reconciliation, not silent replacement. The appellate opinion recounts $1,944,948 total income reported to the IRS in 2015: that is not a full-year customer-revenue or spending ledger, and the underlying tax return has not been inspected here.

Actual expense evidence—not another forecast

On printed pp780–781 (PDF pp121–122), controller So-Han Spivey discusses consolidated income statements admitted as Defence Exhibit 4176. She gives operating expenses/R&D of $28.2m/$22m for 2011, about $67m/$53m for 2012 and about $92m/$68m for 2013, agreeing significant R&D contributed to operating losses. This is evidence of substantial research expenditure, not proof all funding vanished or went on personal extravagance. It is testimony about accounting expenses, not an independent audit or a complete cash ledger; amounts are rounded as stated.

What this does not answer

These records do not allocate all investor receipts between employees, research, equipment, retail operations and legal costs. Capitalised equipment, operating expense, net loss, accumulated deficit, cash payments and court loss measures are not interchangeable. No complete spending total has been reconstructed.
