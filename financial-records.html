Three different statements in one exhibit

Exhibit 15031 contains projected income, proforma cash flow and a company-reported 14 July 2014 balance sheet. The balance sheet uses thousands of dollars; the forecast tables use dollars. A reported balance is not independently audited cash, and neither a deficit nor a forecast expense is a paid-cash ledger.

A court-described counterweight

The opinion recounts controller testimony of $92 million net loss in 2013 and a November 2013 internal account of up to $2 million weekly spending. Those figures are not a period-wide, independently reconstructed cash ledger; do not multiply a weekly rate into an invented total.

Arithmetic discrepancies are questions, not intent findings

The reproduced forecasts contain differences between labelled net-income figures and between displayed closing balances and arithmetic. The checks below use the printed values without silently repairing them. Underlying worksheets, definitions and rounding policies are needed before explaining the differences.

What this does not answer

These records do not allocate all investor receipts between employees, research, equipment, retail operations and legal costs. Capitalised equipment, operating expense, net loss, accumulated deficit, cash payments and court loss measures are not interchangeable. No complete spending total has been reconstructed.
