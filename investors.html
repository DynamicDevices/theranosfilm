03 / Representations and reliance

A future ambition.
Or a present fact?

Risky investment is not the same thing as consent to inaccurate information. But a failed investment is not, by itself, evidence of fraud.

Start with the actual words

Trial Exhibit 3696 contains present-tense technology claims on page 3 and assertions of validation on page 8. Our earlier research visually inspected those pages, not the whole presentation in context. A description that technology “runs” tests is different from a proposal that it “might eventually run” them.

That distinction is a starting point, not a complete legal conclusion. We still need the recipient, delivery date, full packet, accompanying statements and relevant technology at that time. Do not assume every investor saw the same materials.

What information formed the investment case?

The SEC complaint, paragraphs 48–51, describes meetings, demonstrations and materials including slides, reports and forecasts. These are allegations in that pleading. Each should be checked against exhibits and testimony rather than silently promoted to a finding.

A useful comparison has five columns: the exact representation, its date, its recipient, contemporaneous capability evidence and any qualification or contradiction. Forecasts belong in a different category from statements of historical revenue or completed validation.

Why did people invest?

Several explanations are plausible: belief in the medical opportunity, expectations of scalability, confidence in leadership, respected introductions or apparent third-party validation. These are research hypotheses. For a named investor, establish their stated reasoning through testimony, correspondence or attributable interviews.

Celebrity, wealth or sophistication is not a substitute for access to technical evidence. Equally, an investor’s retrospective account may need comparison with contemporaneous records. We should examine what diligence was requested, permitted, completed and relied upon—not simply judge investors for the outcome.

Keep the verdict precise

The DOJ’s January 2022 account reports convictions on the investor-fraud conspiracy and three substantive investor counts, acquittals on several patient-related counts, and no unanimous verdict on three other investor counts. It is a prosecution account of a verdict, not the full judgment or an analysis of every later appeal.

The mixed outcome matters. Do not say Holmes was convicted of every allegation. Acquittal on particular counts also does not establish that every clinical result was sound. Criminal liability, clinical quality and the truth of a particular film assertion are distinct questions.

The defence qualification belongs in the analysis

Balwani’s May 2026 Supreme Court petition, printed page 12, quotes a December 2013 military/medevac statement as future potential. Earlier research identified this as a material qualification. It is defence advocacy, not a Supreme Court finding; we have not inspected the complete original audio. It should not be omitted because it complicates a simple account, or treated as proving that all other representations were future-oriented.

What would change the analysis?

An original recording showing a clearly future-oriented statement may qualify a selected quotation. A contemporaneous demonstration of the represented capability may support it. Evidence that a demonstration substituted a different machine may undermine it. All deserve the same exact-date, full-context treatment.
