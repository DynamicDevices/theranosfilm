Which investor-facing document was identified?

On printed transcript pp4684–4689 (PDF pp104–109), Peterson identified Exhibit 1853 as financials received from Theranos in a diligence binder, bearing her annotations, and discussed with Holmes and Balwani in October 2014. Testimony addresses $140 million projected 2014 revenue, $990 million projected 2015 revenue, $230 million projected 2015 profit and a planned 900-location Walgreens rollout. This identifies a received document through testimony; the exhibit itself has not been independently inspected here.

Future forecast versus present starting point

Peterson acknowledged the statement was projected. She explained that receiving a December forecast in October suggested the company was at or close to the projection. That interpretation is witness evidence, not a rule that forecasts are guarantees. Existing capability and business-condition claims must be tested separately; a missed forecast alone is not proof of fraud. Keep the SEC's allegations distinct from the DOJ-reported criminal investor-fraud verdict.

Who actually invested?

On printed p4717 (PDF p137), cross-examination identifies Dynasty Financial II LLC as the investing entity. Peterson said she did not know all its beneficial owners. RDV's investment review, the legal investing entity and a shorthand reference to the DeVos family are not interchangeable. The passage does not establish every owner's allocation or reconcile all payments.

A matching number is not proof of delivery

Exhibit 15031 includes a July 2014 internal email and projections containing $230 million 2015 net income. This numerical match does not establish that this exact version was delivered to Peterson, when she received it, its accompanying qualifications or the decisions it influenced.

What this already clarifies

A financial forecast is explicitly about a future period. A claim that a proprietary instrument already performs a test is about present capability. An investment pitch can contain both, and uncertainty in the forecast does not automatically qualify the separate technical representation. Establish actual delivery and reliance before explaining why this investor invested.

Provisional conclusion

This is a useful primary-record case study, not yet a complete reconstruction of a particular investment. It shows why forecast risk, present capability and actual reliance need separate treatment. A matching dollar figure does not close the delivery gap.
