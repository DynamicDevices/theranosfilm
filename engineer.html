The paper itself says integration was incomplete

Printed p65 states that the then-current platform could not contain all modules in one chassis; full functionality in one device was future work. It also acknowledges one cartridge at a time and data for only one assay/panel per cartridge. This narrows the demonstrated platform claim without denying the selected assay results. It does not independently identify the film's device or establish the engineer's intended criticism.

Accuracy after retesting is not first-attempt reliability

Supporting methods disclose invalid runs, retests and exclusions. HSV-2 comparison had 21 runs meeting exclusion criteria, all repeated; lipid comparison had 13, with seven repeated and six runs from four subjects excluded; lymphocyte comparison had seven, with two retested and five subjects excluded. These disclosures are not proof of misconduct. Do not divide by final specimen counts to invent an attempt-failure rate; retained-result accuracy, first-attempt yield, turnaround and cost are separate questions.

The assay demonstrations were not a universal fingerstick workflow

Supporting Table 1 specifies whole blood, serum or plasma according to the assay, with loaded volumes of 30 or 160 microlitres and run times of 45–140 minutes. Zika sensitivity used spiked venous blood; other comparisons used venous serum/plasma or cell samples, some adjusted to abnormal concentrations. These are disclosed experiments, not independent end-to-end fingerstick validation. Loaded volume, protocol consumption and original collection volume must not be conflated.

Which Theranos machine actually tested patients?

The amended appellate opinion states that Edison was the only Theranos-developed device used for patient testing; miniLab and other devices remained in development. It distinguishes 300 developed small-sample assays from twelve ever run on Edison. This is the court's selected account of trial evidence, not an independently reconstructed assay inventory. A later prototype demonstration cannot establish earlier deployed capability.

The 2018 company-authored engineering paper describes miniLab hardware, assay-configurable cartridges and a connected server called the virtual analyzer. A tiny sample must still be representative

Bond and Richards-Kortum found greater drop-to-drop variability in several cellular measurements from fingerprick blood than in venous controls, with improved consistency at larger sampled volumes in their experiment. This was not a Theranos-machine evaluation and does not show all fingerstick tests fail. Collection and preparation require validation alongside the analyser; a better sensor does not by itself establish representative sampling.

What technological contrast can be established?

The SEC complaint describes the earlier Theranos analyser as limited to immunochemistry and the proposed miniLab as an attempt to integrate more test methods; it alleges the broader device was not clinically ready at launch. These records explain a contrast, not the unidentified speaker's verified argument.

Did any Theranos technology work?

FDA K143236 cleared a specific HSV-1 IgG assay in July 2015 for specified uses and specimens, including fingerstick whole blood. The 7 July letter corrects a 2 July letter. This is a genuine, limited regulatory achievement—not clearance of a universal platform or validation of every prior claim. The blanket statement that nothing worked is too broad.

Independent evidence about the delivered service

A 2016 JCI study compared 22 measurements in 60 healthy adults across Theranos, Quest and LabCorp. It found significant interservice differences and more out-of-range flags at Theranos. This is independent published evidence of service-level concerns, not proof every Theranos result was wrong, a device teardown or validation of every assay and patient population.

What would make the criticism testable?

Separate sample preparation, assay chemistry, optical detection, instrument integration and business economics. Raman spectroscopy examines light scattered by molecular vibrations; translating spectra into a reliable clinical result still needs calibration, interference testing and independent clinical validation. An alternative detection method does not by itself solve all the sample or validation problems. This is a general technical explanation, not the unidentified speaker's verified argument.
